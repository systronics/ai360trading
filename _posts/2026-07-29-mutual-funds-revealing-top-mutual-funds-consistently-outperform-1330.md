## The Numbers That Actually Matter
When it comes to evaluating mutual funds, there are a few key numbers that matter. The expense ratio is a critical metric, as it directly impacts the investor's returns. A lower expense ratio can make a significant difference over the long term. For example, if you invest Rs. 10,000 per month in a fund with an expense ratio of 1.5%, you'll end up paying around Rs. 1,500 per year in fees. In contrast, a fund with an expense ratio of 0.5% will cost you only Rs. 500 per year. This may not seem like a lot, but it can add up over time. Another essential metric is the fund's AUM, as it can impact the fund's ability to generate returns. A fund with a large AUM may struggle to generate significant returns, while a smaller fund may have more flexibility to invest in a wider range of assets. Rolling returns are also crucial, as they provide a more accurate picture of a fund's performance over time. By focusing on these numbers, investors can make more informed decisions and increase their chances of finding top mutual funds that consistently outperform SIP expectations now.
