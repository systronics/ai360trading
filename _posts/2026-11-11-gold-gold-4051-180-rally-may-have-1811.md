## Step-by-Step  
Below is a beginner‑friendly checklist. Follow the numbers in order; you can pause at any point to double‑check your numbers or research a specific vehicle.  

1. **Set your risk horizon** – Ask yourself: *Am I saving for a 5‑year emergency fund or a 20‑year retirement corpus?* Gold’s price can swing 10‑15% in a year, so the longer your horizon, the less you’ll need to worry about short‑term dips.  

2. **Determine allocation** – Most advisors suggest 5‑10% of a diversified portfolio in precious metals. In my view, 7% hits a sweet spot for Indian investors who already hold equity, debt, and a fixed deposit (FD). This figure comes from the “gold vs equities vs FD” debate where gold provides a non‑correlated buffer.  

3. **Choose the vehicle** –  
   - **Physical gold** – Bars or coins, stored at home or in a bank locker. No counter‑party risk, but you pay making‑and‑storage charges.  
   - **Digital gold** – Purchasable on platforms like Zerodha or Groww; you own a ledger entry that can be redeemed for physical metal. Liquidity is high, and you avoid storage hassles.  
   - **Gold ETF** – Tracks the price of gold, listed on NSE/BSE. It offers instant exposure and can be bought via a demat account.  
   - **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)** – Issued by the RBI, these carry a 2.5% annual interest (tax‑free) plus the gold price appreciation. I covered the nuances in a piece earlier this week – see the link for a deeper dive.  

4. **Check the price level** – Key technical levels for gold today:  
   - **$4,030** – A short‑term support zone that held during the March 2020 pandemic sell‑off.  
   - **$4,080** – A resistance line that was tested after the July 2024 Fed pause.  
   - **$4,200** – A longer‑term psychological barrier; a clear break could signal a new bull phase.  

   If gold breaks below $4,030, expect a test of the $3,950 level (the March 2020 low). A move above $4,080 would suggest the market is pricing in a softer Fed stance.  

5. **Factor in currency impact** – With USD/INR at 93.68, every 1‑point rise in the rupee reduces the INR cost of a dollar‑denominated metal. If you buy SGBs, you lock in the rupee price, which can be advantageous when the rupee weakens.  

6. **Allocate funds** – Example: You have Rs.3 lakh earmarked for a balanced portfolio. At a 7% gold allocation, you would invest Rs.21,000 in gold. Split it: Rs.10,000 in digital gold for liquidity, Rs.5,000 in an SGB for interest, and Rs.6,000 in a Gold ETF for market exposure.  

7. **Monitor macro cues** – Keep an eye on:  
   - Fed minutes (look for “patient” language).  
   - RBI inflation bulletin (food CPI).  
   - US 10‑year yield moves (spreads over Indian 10‑year G‑Sec).  

8. **Rebalance annually** – If gold’s share drifts to 12% after a rally, sell a portion to bring it back to 7%. This discipline prevents over‑exposure during a bull run.

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Chasing the rally** – Buying gold just because it jumped $180 in a day (the recent rally from $3,871 to $4,051) often leads to buying at a local top. I learned this the hard way in 2019 when I added to a gold position after a sudden price spike and watched it retrace 8% within weeks.  

2. **Ignoring the rupee factor** – Many Indian investors focus on the dollar price and forget that a weakening INR can offset a dip in gold’s USD value.  

3. **Over‑allocating** – Some forums push 20% of net worth into gold. That ignores the non‑correlated benefits of debt and equities.  

4. **Holding only physical gold** – Storage, insurance, and purity verification costs erode returns.  

5. **Neglecting tax implications** – Long‑term capital gains on physical gold above Rs.2 lakh are taxed at 20% with indexation, while SGBs enjoy tax‑free interest and capital gains if held till maturity.
