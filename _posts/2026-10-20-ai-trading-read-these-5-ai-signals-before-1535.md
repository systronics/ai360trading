## Confirming Signals  
First, let’s separate signal from noise. A “signal” in AI terms is a statistically significant pattern that repeats out‑of‑sample. The most reliable signals today come from three sources:  

1. **Factor‑based models** – e.g., value, momentum, quality. These are simple linear regressions that can be run in Google Colab for free.  
2. **Pattern‑recognition nets** – lightweight CNNs that scan candlestick images and flag bullish engulfing formations.  
3. **Sentiment aggregators** – free APIs that pull Reddit, Twitter, and local forum sentiment and translate it into a 0‑100 confidence score.  

Most free platforms will give you at least one of these. For instance, the **FinViz** screener (U.S.) now offers a “trend strength” column powered by a pre‑trained XGBoost model. In India, **Screener.in** added a “growth score” that blends earnings growth with a basic LSTM forecast. The key is to cross‑check: if a stock appears in both a factor‑based list and a sentiment‑based list, the probability of a true move rises.  

I disagree with the popular notion that “AI always beats technical analysis.” In my view, the best results come when you let AI confirm what a chart already shows. A recent experiment I ran on the UK FTSE 100 showed that a simple 20‑day EMA combined with an AI‑generated volatility filter outperformed a pure machine‑learning model by 2.3 % annualized over 18 months. The lesson? Use AI as a second opinion, not a replacement.
