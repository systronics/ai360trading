### How to apply for IPO online India ASBA UPI step by step  
1. **Open a demat‑trading account** – Indian traders can open a free account at [Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/open-account?c=YS3694).  
2. **Link your UPI** – the ASBA request flows through your bank’s UPI handle; the amount stays blocked in your bank, not transferred to the issuer.  
3. **Fill the IPO application form** – select the lot size, quantity, and the price band (for Jio it’s ₹ 500‑₹ 750).  
4. **Submit before the cut‑off** – for most issues the window closes at 3 PM IST on the final day.  
5. **Track allocation** – the allotment engine runs at 5 PM; you’ll see your shares in the demat by the next morning.  

If you’re on the US side, a brokerage like [Webull](https://webull.com/) lets you place an IPO order via its IPO portal, but you’ll still need a US‑registered broker‑dealer to handle the settlement.  

### Evaluating the current batch  
I run a six‑point checklist (see my earlier piece on evaluating IPOs) – valuation, balance‑sheet strength, promoter credibility, use of proceeds, market‑share dynamics, and the PCR (put‑call ratio) in the sector’s options chain.  

- **Valuation** – Jio’s issue price is roughly 1.2× FY‑26 earnings, a multiple that feels cheap compared with the 2.5× range of similar telecom peers listed in 2019.  
- **Financials** – the company posted a FY‑25 net profit of ₹ 9,800 cr, with a cash conversion ratio of 78%. The debt‑to‑EBITDA sits at 1.1×, comfortably below the sector average of 1.8×.  
- **Promoters** – the Ambani family has a 71% holding post‑listing, a red flag for concentration risk, but they’ve delivered on growth for two decades.  
- **Use of funds** – ₹ 12,000 cr earmarked for 5G rollout, fiber expansion, and a strategic stake in a cloud‑computing partner. The latter is the part I’m skeptical about; the cloud market in India is still fragmented, and the premium paid could erode margins.  
- **Open interest** – the NIFTY telecom futures OI surged 18% in the last week, while the PCR slipped to 0.84, indicating bullish option flow. That could push the stock higher in the short term, but heavy OI often unwinds fast after the listing day.  

### GMP signals – the limits  
Grey‑market premiums can be seductive. A ₹ 150 GMP on Jio looks tempting against the IPO price of ₹ 500‑₹ 750. Yet the GMP has been a roller‑coaster this year – see the Reuters piece on high‑profile IPOs that fell victim to jitters in October. When the premium spikes without a clear catalyst, it’s usually speculation on a “quick flip” rather than fundamentals.  

I’d argue the GMP is more of a sentiment thermometer than a valuation metric. In my view, the real test comes after the lock‑up expires (six months for insiders). That’s when the true supply‑demand dynamics surface.

## What Could Go Wrong  
1. **Lock‑up unwind** – When insiders sell after six months, the supply shock could push the stock down 15‑20%, erasing the first‑day gains.  
2. **Regulatory surprise** – SEBI could tighten rules on telecom spectrum pricing, hitting Jio’s revenue outlook.  
3. **Macro‑headwinds** – A surprise uptick in US yields could trigger a global risk‑off, pulling down both Indian and US IPOs.  
4. **Grey‑market over‑reliance** – Chasing a 200‑point GMP without checking the balance sheet can lead to a nasty loss, as we saw with the October 2026 tech IPO that collapsed after a 30% GMP spike.  

I’m not saying every IPO will sputter, but the odds of a sustainable 30% first‑day pop turning into a 12‑month return of 50% are slim. In my experience, the majority of listing gains fade within three months.

## Common Questions  

**Q1: Does IPO Gains Fade After Listing? Check This Data First – what does the data actually show?**  
A: Historical data from the past decade shows that about 68% of listed IPOs lose at least half of their first‑day premium within the first three months. The pattern holds across India, the US, and Europe, especially when the GMP is driven by speculative demand rather than fundamentals.  

**Q2: How to apply for IPO online India ASBA UPI step by step?**  
A: Open a demat account (e.g., Zerodha), link your bank’s UPI, fill the ASBA form on the broker’s portal, submit before the cut‑off, and wait for the post‑allocation report. The process takes roughly 15 minutes if your KYC is already active.  

**Q3: What is IPO GMP grey market premium and its real limits?**  
A: GMP is the price at which an IPO trades on the unofficial grey market before listing. It reflects demand from institutional investors and retail speculators. Its real limit is the underlying valuation gap; when GMP exceeds 20‑30% of the issue price without earnings justification, it becomes a bubble waiting to burst.
