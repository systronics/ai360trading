Why a 50% Loss Needs a 100% Gain (The Math Every Trader Must Know)

The rule to remember
Rule: decide your maximum loss before you enter, keep it small, usually 1 percent of your account or less per trade, and let the arithmetic protect you.

The lesson, in words

A fifty percent loss needs a one hundred percent gain just to get back to even. This is not an opinion, it is arithmetic, and it explains why serious traders think about losses before they think about profits.

Start with one hundred. Lose fifty percent and you have fifty left. To get back to one hundred you must double that fifty, which is a gain of 100 percent. The loss and the gain are not the same size. The deeper the hole, the steeper the climb.

Here is the pattern. A 10 percent loss needs an 11.1 percent gain to recover. A 20 percent loss needs 25.0. A 30 percent loss needs 42.9. A 50 percent loss needs 100.0. And a 70 percent loss needs 233.3 percent. Notice how the bars bend upward. After about thirty percent, recovery becomes very hard.

Now see why risk per trade matters. Every trader eventually loses ten trades in a row. If you risk 1 percent per trade, ten losses leave you down 9.6 percent. Risk 2 percent, and you are down 18.3 percent. Risk 5 percent, down 40.1 percent. Risk 10 percent per trade, and you are down 65.1 percent, which needs a 186.5 percent gain just to recover.

What this does not prove. The arithmetic is exact, but it does not tell you whether your system has an edge. Real markets add gaps and slippage, so an actual loss can be larger than the one you planned. Rule: decide your maximum loss before you enter, keep it small, usually 1 percent of your account or less per trade, and let the arithmetic protect you. This is education, not investment advice.
