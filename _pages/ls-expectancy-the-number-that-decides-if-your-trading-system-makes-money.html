Expectancy: The Number That Decides If Your Trading System Makes Money

The rule to remember
Rule: judge a system by its expectancy and the size of its losses, not by its win rate, and always test it with costs included.

The lesson, in words

Most traders chase a high win rate. The number that really decides whether a system makes money is called expectancy. It tells you how much you earn, on average, every time you take a trade.

Expectancy equals win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss. It is one line of arithmetic. If the result is positive, the system makes money over many trades. If it is negative, the system loses, however good it feels on the days it wins.

Here are our own numbers, from a 5 year test of 2452 trades on 194 large Indian stocks. Only 41 percent of the trades won. But the average win was 16.8 percent and the average loss was 6.2 percent. So 0.41 times 16.8 is 6.9, and 0.59 times 6.2 is 3.7. The difference is about 3.2 percent per trade. The measured average was 3.3 percent, and the small gap is rounding.

Now the trap. A system that wins 70 percent of the time, with an average win of 2 percent and an average loss of 6 percent, has an expectancy of -0.4 percent per trade. It wins most days and still loses money. A coin flip with wins and losses of 5 percent each sits at exactly 0.

What this does not prove. These numbers come from past data on stocks that survived to today, they ignore brokerage and slippage, and one very strong year pulled the average up, so the future may not look the same. Rule: judge a system by its expectancy and the size of its losses, not by its win rate, and always test it with costs included. This is education, not investment advice.
